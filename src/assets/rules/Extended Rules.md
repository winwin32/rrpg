# Core Mechanic
Player characters can attempt anything in Reforged RPG.

If the action is impossible, the action fails, and the Game Master determines the consequence of failure. 

If the action is possible and is difficult, dangerous, or has a time constraint, roll the ten-sided-die plus any bonuses against a target number. 

- If the roll is equal to or higher than the target number, the player succeeds. 

- The **Bonus Difference** is a number equal to the target number, subtracted by any bonuses to the roll.

- If the roll is equal to or higher than the **Bonus Difference** but lower than the target number, it is a **Near Miss** 

- If the roll is lower than the **Bonus Difference** it is a failure, and the Game Master determines the consequence of failure.

If the roll is possible and is **not** difficult, dangerous, or time-constrained, the action succeeds.

# Attributes 

There are 3 attributes in Reforged RPG, **Body**, **Mind** and **Soul**

**Body** represents the character's physical speed, strength, and grace 

**Mind** represents the character's wit, memory, and attention to detail

**Soul** represents the character's connection to people, nature, and the divine

Most bonuses for rolls will use one of these attributes.

Each character starts off with a base 5 in each attribute, but only numbers higher or lower than five will affect rolls. 

6 body gives a +1 to body rolls, 4 mind gives -1 to mind rolls, etc.

# Encounters

An **Encounter** is any situation with inherent danger or the party's desired outcome is not certain.

An encounter must have objectives and bonus objectives. 

An **objective** is something the party wants to accomplish, and there can be more than one objective in an encounter. 

### Example Objectives:

- Scatter the goblin raiding party 

- Flee the rock-throwing trolls 

- Convince the Baron to divorce his dangerous wife 

- Scale the icy cliff during a blizzard 

A **bonus objective** is something an individual party member wants to accomplish other than the main objective while in this encounter. 

Bonus objectives always award **Class Points** (See Character Progression for more details) 

### Example Bonus Objectives:

- An honorable knight - Make sure none of the villagers die in the gnoll attack. 

- A tinkering wizard - Make sure to capture a bit of the dragon’s fiery breath before the meatheads kill it. 

- A greedy thief - Make sure to steal the Crown of the Mermaid’s Vengeance in the chaos of the kraken attack. 

When starting an **encounter**, follow these steps:

**Step 1:**

Determine an **objective** (For the entire party)

**Step 2:**

Determine **Bonus Objectives** (For individual players)

**Step 3:**

Opposing forces make starting moves.

**Step 4:**

The Game Master selects a player to begin taking their turn. 

After their turn is over, they will select another player until all have taken their turn this round. 

**Step 5:**

Opposing forces finish their turn

Repeat from Step 3

# Action Tokens

**Action Tokens** represent the player's mental physical and spiritual energy. 

Any action taken within an **encounter** costs 1 **Action Token**, regardless of whether it required a roll. 

A player can use up to 3 **Action Tokens** on one turn. 

Whenever **Action Tokens** are used, they are pulled from the **Action Pool**.

The **Action Pool** replenishes whenever the character gets ~~an adequate rest.~~

The ~~symbol~~ indicates this is left up to the Game Master's discretion; they can decide what best fits the adventure.

# Abilities 

An **Ability** is a pre-defined action a player can take. 

Like actions, abilities cost 1 **Action Token** while used inside encounters and sometimes require rolls. 

If an action falls into the ability's trigger statement, it must follow the rules of the ability. 

Abilities start as **Whole**, can be **Broken**, and then can be **Reforged** through enemy and ally actions. 

**Whole** abilities usually work as described.

**Broken** abilities usually do not function. Their listed "broken" effect will occur when they are broken.

**Reforged** abilities usually function just like **Whole** abilities. Their listed "reforged" effect will occur when they are reforged.

There are exceptions to all of the above 3 statements, depending on the specific ability description. That will override the above rules. 

Whole abilities can be Reforged if desired. 

Some abilities are passive and never require an **Action Token** to activate their effects.

The caster will lose any conditions associated with passive abilities if that ability breaks but keep any items or social contexts it grants. 

Effects that happen when abilities break or are reforged never cost action tokens. 

**Secondary Abilities** cannot be broken. (See the Ability List for more details) 

**Primary Abilities** are Attack, Move, Flee, Recover, in addition to all the abilities gained from experience points. 

Abilities with a “target” require **Line of Sight**, which means one point of the caster’s hex needs to be able to connect to a point on the target hex via a straight line.

# Combat Encounters

Starting a combat encounter includes the same 4 steps as any other encounter:

**Step 1:**

Determine an **objective** (For the entire party)

**Step 2:**

Determine **Bonus Objectives** (For individual players)

**Step 3:**

Enemies move 

**Step 4:**

The Game Master selects a player to begin taking their turn. 

This player spends up to 3 **Action Tokens** then ends their turn. 

After their turn is over, they will select another player until all have taken their turn this round. 

**Step 5:**

Enemies use abilities. 

All enemies in range attack. 

Repeat from step 3 

## Fighting and Dying 
Attacking an enemy will cost 1 **Action Token** and 1 **Attack Token**. All players receive 1 **Attack Token** at the start of their turn, but can gain more through abilities. 

Attack tokens can’t stack if a player didn’t use them in the previous turn. 

They are used in Attack abilities, which are triggered by causing harm to an enemy in any way. 

Successfully attacking an enemy or being attacked in turn **Breaks** 1 ability. 

The defender chooses which ability to break. 

If all of a character or enemy's primary abilities are **Broken** they become **Vulnerable**.

The next attack one takes while vulnerable kills them, no roll required. 

There is no concept of health or hit points in Reforged RPG, to stay alive one must prevent their abilities from being broken and becoming vulnerable. 

Ability rolls always succeed against vulnerable targets

**Base Threat** is a number representing how dangerous an enemy is. 

**Total Threat** is equal to the base threat of all enemies in range to attack at the start of Step 5 of the combat encounter.

All enemies then attack on Step 5, not requiring a roll.

**Party Defense** is determined by adding a character’s toughness to the total every time a character is attacked. 

**Total Threat** is then subtracted from **Party Defense** to equal total attacks that hit the party for that round. 

Those total attacks are split evenly between characters targeted that round by the Game Master.

## Tactics 

Characters can interact with the environment to get bonuses to their attacks, such as high ground, flanking, dropping heavy objects, knocking enemies off of high places, and ~~anything else the players can imagine~~ 

When an ally is attacked, if another character is adjacent, the subject of the attack can **Shift** the attack to them. 

**Shifting** an attack prevents any effect of the attack on the original target and makes their ally the target. 

Shifting can only happen with the permission of both players. 

Shifting does not cost an action token 

Lockdown is a scaling effect that makes movement around the subject more difficult. 

Stacking lockdown can help protect more vulnerable party members by controlling areas of the battlefield. 

Lockdown is a condition, **Conditions** are effects added by abilities. 

Effects added by passive abilities will remain in effect until that passive ability breaks. 

Effects added by active abilities expire after being “used”.

What that means precisely for each condition is detailed in the **Conditions** section.

**Large** combatants cannot be pushed and gain +1 to the Body attribute. 

## The Battlefield 
Combat Encounters can happen anywhere. 

The battlefield is represented in hexes, each point of movement a player has represents the number of hexes they can move when spending 1 **Action Token**. 

A honeycomb is 6 hexes surrounding a middle hex. 

If a hex is 1 hex above another, it represents a steep slope or a tough but simple climb. It takes an additional hex of movement to move upward as well as onto this hex. 

If a character or enemy is one hex above another, they gain *Empowered x1* as from a passive ability. They will lose this condition if they no longer have this height difference. 

Swimming or moving in difficult terrain costs 2 hexes of movement rather than 1.

# Social Encounters 

A **Social Encounter** begins when a social interaction necessary to the party's desired outcome is not certain.

When the party makes a request of a person, that person will have **Positive Motivators** and **Negative Motivators**

To convince the other person in a social situation, they must have more positive than negative motivators. 

The starting motivators will be determined by **Social Context**. 

**Social Context** is a basic detail of a character’s life that describes their background or connections to the people around them. 

### Example Social Contexts:

- Member of the Dwarven Silver Hammer Clan

- Versed in Demonic Lore 

- Really ugly 

### Example Positive Motivators 

- The party greeted the governor with the proper ancestral greeting 

- The delegate thinks the knight is very dashing 

- The party is offering a nice bribe if the governor lets them 

### Example Negative Motivators 

- The elf maiden is disgusted that a dwarf is speaking to her 

- The dryad is furious that the party cut down a tree for firewood last night 

- The librarian is scared the king will punish him if he lets the party read the forbidden tome 

Most motivators begin the encounter as hidden but are revealed as the person and the party take actions. 

The social abilities every player has access to are Demonstrate, Inquire, Expound, and Persuade. (See ability list for more details) 

These abilities can add positives or remove negatives 

The party can be deceitful at any point in a social encounter, as well as the other person, but both parties will need to be careful to keep their facts straight. 

These are the steps to follow for social encounters 

**Step 1:** 
Objective

**Step 2:**
Bonus Objectives

**Step 3:**
Any obvious/previously established context is added for positive and negative

Other person/party may reveal 1 negative motivator

**Step 4:**
Players take actions

**Step 5:**
Other person/party either accepts, refuses, or allows negotiation to continue

Repeat from Step 3

# Tension Pool

The **Tension Pool** represents the rising tension and the danger of lingering in hostile places. 

The **tension pool** is active while the party is somewhere unsafe and unknown. 

If the party does something time-consuming, add a 6-sided die to the **tension pool**. 

If the party does something reckless, roll the **tension pool**. 

If the party does something reckless and time-consuming, do both. 

Upon adding the 6th die to the pool, roll the **tension pool** and clear it. 

# Travel 

On a world map, each day of travel by foot is represented by 1 map-hex. 

==hex-map.png==

Every time a party crosses a hex, they must Navigate. 

They may also Scout and Forage (See Ability List for details on all 3)

Each one may be easier or more difficult depending on the environment.

### Comfort

Whenever **Action Tokens** are used in an encounter, they are pulled from the **Action Pool**.

The amount of **Action Tokens** in an **Action Pool** is equal to the party's **Comfort**

Party **Comfort** represents the ease and comfort with which the party traveled the previous day. 

**Comfort** will be affected by different factors throughout the day, then when the party rests, they will get an allotment of **Action Tokens** based on that day's comfort to use when they awake. 

### Table of how different factors can influence Comfort:

| Action | Comfort |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------: |
| Travel slowly | +1 |
| Eat lots of food | +1 |
| Eat expensive food | +1 |
| Sleep somewhere comfortable | +1 |
| Carousing at a local tavern | +1 |
| Wear armor or heavy weapons at all times | -1 |
| Keep a nightly watch | -1 |
| Move quickly | -1 |
| Have full carrying capacity | -1 |
| Traveling in bad weather | -1 |

### Carrying Capacity 
Characters can carry units of weight equal to their Body attribute. 

A unit is a very simplified measure of weight, roughly 10-20 pounds (5-10 kilos).

### Example cargo and their weight:

| Item | Weight |
| ------------------------------ | ------: |
| 1 Food | 1 unit |
| 1 Water | 1 unit |
| 1 Potion | 1 unit |
| Chest full of gold and jewelry | 2 units |
| Plate armor | 2 units |

# Companions 

**Minions** are characters that a player can control as a result of different abilities. 

Minions have Attack and Move as secondary abilities, but can only act using the caster’s action and **Attack Tokens**. 

Minions act on the same turn as their caster. 

Minions have the following statistics:
- 3 movement
- 3 toughness 
- 3 body 
- 3 mind 
- 3 soul 

Minions do not share conditions with the caster. 

While at least 1 minion is active, the caster can use an additional **Action Token** per turn. 

Attacks cannot normally be shifted to minions. 

### Mounts

While mounted, the rider and Mount are treated as a single entity for targeting and action rules.

Therefore enemies cannot choose to attack the mount or rider, and the pair only takes one turn.

The pair gains Empowered x2

The pair shares all abilities

The pair can spend 4 Action Tokens per turn

Anytime the rider dismounts they lose all benefits

The ability that summoned the mount when broken, causes the rider to dismount and lose all benefits

Outside of combat, mounts have 5 carrying capacity and if an entire party has mounts they can move 2 map-hexes instead of 1 per day.

# Character Progression 

When first creating a character, players get 5 experience points and choose one **Foreshadowed Ability**. (See Character Creation section for more details)

To level up, characters need to complete the main objective of 3 encounters.

All characters receive 2 experience points to spend every level up. They also receive their **Foreshadowed Ability** for free.

**Class points** represent specific experience with a skill or source of power. 

You will gain **Class Points** from **Bonus Objectives**, and you need a certain threshold to unlock higher-level abilities.

### Determine Characteristics 

Write one or more characteristics for your character linked to each unique class point added. 

Even if a character doesn’t have a characteristic for every quirk or personality trait, they can still act however the player likes. 

Characteristics should just be the most visible or recognizable driving force behind the character’s decisions.

**Example Characteristics:** 

Matchstick likes to blow things up! (Elemental power) 

John protects any helpless people that remind him of his daughter. (Heavy prowess)

Windstrider believes that anyone with green eyes has been possessed by the fae (Nature Spirit knowledge) 

**In encounters, the above characters will get bonus objectives like:**

Blow up 2 enemies at once, because Matchstick likes to blow things up! 

Protect the dryad because John protects any helpless people that remind him of his daughter. 

Never turn your back on the green-eyed archer because Windstrider believes that anyone with green eyes has been possessed by the fae
